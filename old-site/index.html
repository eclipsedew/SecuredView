What happens

Three moves, start to finish.

A VPN is not complicated, and a company that explains it plainly has less room to mislead you. This is the whole of it.

01

The handshake

1.2 ms, keys held in memory

Your device and the exit node agree on a set of keys that only the two of them hold. The keys are new for every session and are never written to disk, so there is nothing to recover from a seized machine.

02

The tunnel

ChaCha20 Poly1305

Everything leaving your device is sealed inside that tunnel. Whoever runs the network you are sitting on can see that you opened a connection to SecuredView. They cannot see what you asked for, or who you asked.

03

The exit

9 locations worldwide

Traffic leaves from whichever node you picked and continues to its destination in the clear, as it always would. The site you reached sees the node, not you, and we hold nothing that connects the two.

On that last row

We count connections and bytes per node so we know when to add capacity. The counter is a single number per node per minute. It carries no address, no session, and no way to pull one person back out of it. Saying we collect nothing at all would be an easier sentence to write and a false one.

Questions

The ones worth asking a VPN service.

Is it really free?

Yes. Two servers — US East (New York) and Japan (Tokyo) — with full ChaCha20 Poly1305 encryption, no ads, no account needed. Premium adds more locations and priority speeds, but the free tier is a real VPN, not a demo meant to push you toward paying.

What do I get with Premium?

Nine server locations worldwide (US East, US West, UK London, Germany Frankfurt, Japan Tokyo, Singapore, Australia Sydney, Brazil Sao Paulo, India Mumbai), support for up to 3 devices, and priority speeds. Plans start at $3.99 per 30 days. All plans include the same encryption and no-logs policy as the free tier.

What does no logs actually mean here?

It means the eight rows in the ledger above, and nothing hidden behind them. We hold per node counters and nothing else. The privacy page lists what those counters contain, field by field.

Is the speed or the data limited?

No. There is no cap, no throttle after a threshold, and no slow queue you get put in. Free and premium users share the same network infrastructure. Premium users get priority routing during congestion.

Which country's laws apply?

The company is registered in Ghana and the network is operated from Accra. Ghana has no data retention mandate covering this kind of service. Nodes in other countries are subject to local law where they sit, which is one reason they run from memory and hold nothing.

What happens when a court asks for my traffic?

We answer, because refusing to answer a valid order is not a strategy. What we hand over is what we have, which is a counter showing how many connections a node carried in a given minute. We publish the count of requests received and the count answered with data twice a year.

Can I run it on my router?

Yes. Configuration files for the standard client work on most routers that support it, and the setup page walks through the common ones. Nothing about the router path is second class.
